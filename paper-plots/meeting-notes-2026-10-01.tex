{\Large Neural DNO meeting notes}\hfill October 1, 2026

\textbf{Main message.} Inference is now fast enough to compete with low-order
Craig--Sulem evaluation, but we do not yet have a reliable accuracy advantage.
The phase-focused recipes improved Stokes substantially and hurt Tanaka. Our next
tests target depth conditioning and incomplete supervision of the operator.

\textbf{Accuracy.} The new recipes improve Stokes by about $4.5\times$ versus
the original EMA model, but neither beats M3's median in any family. Modal phase
improves three medians versus local tangent while worsening all four
95th-percentile errors. Tanaka's 95th percentile rises from $4.64\%$ (original)
to $7.67\%$ (local) and $11.19\%$ (modal).
Both new recipes also changed the data normalization; the Stokes improvement
cannot be attributed to phase loss alone.

\textbf{Runtime.} On Ada, the original optimized EMA model takes
$1.25$--$1.29$ seconds for $T=20$ and $12.33$--$12.62$ seconds for $T=200$.
M2 takes approximately $0.97/9.6$ seconds and M3 $1.57/15.7$ seconds.
The model sits between them in cost, but generally not in accuracy.
These are warm, full single rollouts, not batch-throughput estimates.

\subsection*{What the diagnostics suggest}
\textbf{Phase drift matters, but its loss is not a complete fix.}
Earlier alignment diagnostics attributed most Stokes/Tanaka trajectory error
to translation. The completed experiments show that targeting phase can improve
one regime while making steep Tanaka tails worse.

\textbf{A stronger lead: learning the operator beyond the observed potential.}
At fixed $(\eta,h)$, changing only the phases of $\xi$ while preserving its
Fourier amplitudes raises the original model's paired median one-step error
by $23\times$ on Stokes and $119\times$ on Benjamin--Feir.
All three models beat M2 on all 32 original BF inputs but on none of the
32 scrambled-input case medians. This suggests inadequate coverage of
Dirichlet directions; it does not yet establish the cause of rollout drift.

\textbf{Depth conditioning remains plausible.}
Adding $\eta/h$ improved all four fresh one-epoch median rollout errors,
but worsened Tanaka's 95th percentile. Gauge centering made little difference;
learned surface filters were mixed. Positivity held on the four audited hard
Tanaka surfaces, so positivity enforcement is not our first intervention.

\subsection*{Running now and next decision}
As of 03:22 local time, the $\eta/h$ candidate has completed 7 of 120 epochs;
its otherwise identical target-loss control is queued on GPU 1.
Rollouts are scheduled at epochs 10, 30, 60, and 120.

The separate fresh one-epoch pilot tests \textbf{CS-labelled potential probes}:
keep each surface/depth, retain the original potential, and add a same-spectrum
phase-scrambled potential with its own M6 label. Its paired control is running;
the probe arm follows. Both use LR $2\times10^{-5}$ and receive 128-case rollout
evaluation. This changes training coverage, not inference cost.

\textbf{Decision criterion.} Compare the controlled runs using family medians
\emph{and} tails. Look for gains that survive rollout evaluation without a
Tanaka regression before committing to another long run. Do not promote a
variant solely because its aggregate validation loss is lower.
